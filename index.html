Base Offense

Lions stay guard-driven. Most half-court trips start from the top or slot, then flow into one live-dribble attack, one quick pitch, or one simple angle/drag screen instead of a long chained action.

Shot Profile

Their cleanest offense still comes from paint touches first. The perimeter shot helps the action, but the most reliable points come from slot drives, deep seals, short catches, and second-jump finishes by the bigger bodies.

Late-Clock Pattern

When the first action stalls, the floor tends to flatten. They usually come back to #4 or #14 at the top/right slot and ask for one more decision instead of generating a full weak-side counter.

Best Coverage Rules

ICE or widen side attacks, crowd the first paint touch, and make the second creator beat you. If the first gap closes early, their offense becomes more one-pass-and-watch and their shot quality comes down.

Transition + Glass

#13 and #77 add value through rim runs, seals, and second jumps. If you do not finish the possession with a gang rebound, the Lions can turn a broken trip into paint points without needing a clean first action.

Confirmed Core

Colin

Lead guard / first-touch creator / #4

Confirmed jersey: #4. Main point-of-attack initiator. Confidence is high on role and medium on pure jumper volume.

Primary action: slot initiation Best band: left slot / top Live with: deep right pull-up

Tactical Role

First touch on a large share of half-court trips. He is the player most likely to start from high middle or the left slot, read the first gap, and decide whether the possession becomes a drive, pitch, or reset.

Best Score Areas

Left-slot threes, top-of-arc reset threes, and middle-lane pull/float finishes after the first step wins. The right sideline pull-up looks more like a bailout shot than his preferred diet.

Technical Read

Low pocket dribble into a right-hand burst. Release is compact and quick, more one-motion push than high-lift jumper, and he shoots best when he sees the gap before rising.

Live With / Pressure

Live with the deeper right-side pull-up or the wider sideline escape. His comfort drops when the first lane is crowded and he has to throw late over length instead of stepping cleanly into rhythm.

Coverage Call

Show inside chest, send the first dribble wide, and make him play over a loaded body. If side action comes, ICE it and force the second handler to finish the trip.

Confirmed Core

Josh

Secondary scorer / play-finisher / #1

Confirmed jersey: #1. Off-ball scorer and straight-line finisher. Confidence is high on catch-and-go role and medium on total on-ball creation.

Primary action: catch-and-go Best band: right wing / corner Live with: left-hand traffic

Tactical Role

Second-wave scorer. He benefits when #4 or #14 bend the floor first, then he attacks the catch after the defense has already shifted and cannot load up early.

Best Score Areas

Right wing and right corner feel like the cleanest catch-and-go spaces. Midrange touch looks more comfortable from the right elbow than the left, especially if the closeout is late.

Technical Read

Quick catch-to-rip and straight-line go. The jumper starts from a low dip and rises cleanly without a long set point, so he is most dangerous when the ball arrives on time.

Live With / Pressure

Live with extra left-hand dribbles into traffic and contested mid or long twos. He looks more like a finisher than a long-possession creator when you take away the clean right-side catch.

Coverage Call

Top-lock the easy catch, run him off the right side, and meet him at the second dribble. Make him see bodies instead of a one-step launch or rim line.

Confirmed Core

Elijah

Interior finisher / glass pressure / #13

Confirmed jersey: #13. Interior pressure piece. Confidence is high on rim-run and second-jump value.

Primary action: seal / cleanup Best band: paint / short corner Live with: one-step-up catches

Tactical Role

Rim-run and clean-up big. He is the player most likely to convert effort into points through seals, dump-offs, putbacks, and second jumps instead of through high-volume self-created touches.

Best Score Areas

Restricted area, short corner, and both blocks. He gets more dangerous whenever the ball touches the lane twice in the same possession and the defense has to react on the second bounce.

Technical Read

Two-foot gather, quick inside-hand flip, and power finish. He is not a long wind-up scorer; he wins by catching deep, getting shoulders square fast, and finishing before the defense reloads.

Live With / Pressure

Live with catches a step higher and a crowded chest at the point of contact. His value drops when he has to make a one-dribble decision outside the paint instead of finishing on the move.

Coverage Call

Hit early, keep a body between him and the rim, and gang rebound every miss. Do not let his first miss become his second touch.

Confirmed Core

Yeyo

Screen-and-rebound big / #77

Confirmed jersey: #77. Screen-and-seal big. Confidence is high on contact and rebound value, lower on perimeter volume.

Primary action: screen / deep seal Best band: dunker / restricted Live with: short-roll touch

Tactical Role

Big-body screener and rebound helper. He creates value by absorbing contact, pinning defenders, and turning average entries into deep catches or extra-possession touches.

Best Score Areas

Dunker spot, restricted circle, and short catches directly off contact. Trail threes may exist, but the three-game sample still reads them as surprise shots rather than a designed staple.

Technical Read

Wins with position first. Catch, pivot, shoulder into space, then use a short push finish instead of a high-elevation release. He is more comfortable finishing through the body than facing up.

Live With / Pressure

Live with the short-roll touch above the block or the trail catch. He becomes much less dangerous when the ball finds him higher and he has to decide instead of simply sealing and finishing.

Coverage Call

Tag his seal early, make him catch over length, and do not let the screen turn into a direct deep-post angle. Weak-side help has to arrive before the catch, not after it.

Confirmed Core

Vince

Veteran connector / calming organizer / #14

Confirmed jersey: #14. Veteran organizer and secondary closer. Confidence is high on pace control and medium on late-clock scoring bands.

Primary action: reset / elbow touch Best band: top / right elbow Live with: deep late-clock jumper

Tactical Role

Stabilizer and connector. He often enters once the first action bends the floor, settles the possession, and decides whether it should become an elbow touch, a reset drive, or a late-clock pull-up.

Best Score Areas

Top of key, right slot, and right elbow. He looks most comfortable when he can square the floor first, see both corners, and then attack without rushing the read.

Technical Read

Eyes stay up, handle is upright, and the shot prep is neat. He likes pace dribbles and inside-out rhythm moves more than explosive first-step creation.

Live With / Pressure

Live with deeper late-clock jumpers and left-drifting pull-ups. The efficiency should come down if you make him start earlier and faster than his preferred pace window.

Coverage Call

Crowd the catch, disrupt rhythm before the move starts, and make him operate without the full-floor picture. The goal is to change the tempo, not just contest the release.

Confirmed Core

Ryan

Forward / pressure piece / #40

Confirmed jersey: #40. old-Ryan is #7 and not charted separately yet. Confidence is medium because the offensive role sample is still smaller.

Primary action: lane pressure / glass Best band: lane lines / cleanup Live with: contested perimeter

Tactical Role

Pressure-piece forward. He looks more useful as a cutter, lane runner, and cleanup finisher than as the player asked to create a whole possession from the perimeter.

Best Score Areas

Lane lines, short paint catches, and cleanup range. The threat reads stronger around physical rim pressure than around pure standstill perimeter volume.

Technical Read

Best when playing off movement and contact. The three-game sample still shows more comfort finishing into space than rising from a stationary outside base.

Live With / Pressure

Live with above-the-break threes and contested second-side pull-ups until the bigger sample says otherwise. Do not over-help so hard that the first fake turns into a free lane.

Coverage Call

Help off early outside, tag his rebounding path, and show chest before the catch turns into a downhill step. Keep him a jump shooter, not a rim runner.
