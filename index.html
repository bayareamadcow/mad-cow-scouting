Team Read

Across all three samples, Lions stay guard-driven. Most possessions begin from the top or slot, then flow into a straight-line drive, quick swing, or one simple screen touch rather than a long scripted action.

What Stayed Stable

The same trusted shape keeps showing up late: smaller handlers initiate, then bigger bodies like 13 and 14 bring rim pressure, seals, and extra-possession value. Their easiest points still come when the ball touches the paint early or twice in one trip.

What Game 3 Added

The Pirates game did not really change the read. Even in another black-jersey sample, they still looked more comfortable driving and collapsing the defense than living off complex weak-side motion or pure volume shooting.

Best Pressure Points

Load the point of attack early, push catches wider toward the sideline, and make the second creator finish the possession. When the first lane gets cut off, their weak-side activity can flatten and the half-court offense gets more one-pass-and-watch.

Game Ideas

Defensively, wall the first drive and gang rebound. Offensively, run after misses, hit the second side quickly, and use cuts behind ball-watchers. Make their non-primary handler beat you late in the clock instead of letting the first creator set the rhythm.

Confirmed Core

Colin

Lead guard / first-touch creator / #4

Confirmed jersey: #4. Main point-of-attack initiator in this build.

Tendencies

Starts a lot of possessions from the left slot or high middle, probing with live dribble before deciding whether to score or move it.

Hot Zones

Best reads point to left-slot three, top-of-arc reset threes, and middle-lane pull/float finishes after the first step wins.

First Move / Release

Likes a measured jab or slow bounce into a right-hand burst. Release is compact and fast, closer to a one-motion push than a high-lift jumper.

Weakness

Gets less comfortable when the first lane is crowded and the drive gets pushed wide. The sample is more forgiving from his deeper right-side pull-up and lower-percentage sideline looks than from his left-slot rhythm touches.

How To Guard

Make the first dribble go wide. Sit on the inside lane, crowd the gather, and force him to become a passer over length.

Confirmed Core

Josh

Secondary scorer / play-finisher / #1

Confirmed jersey: #1. Off-ball scorer and straight-line attacker sample.

Tendencies

Profiles like the second wave scorer: catches after the defense shifts, attacks closeouts, and can finish a possession without needing many dribbles.

Hot Zones

Right wing and right corner feel like the cleanest catch-and-go spaces. Midrange touch looks more comfortable from the right elbow than the left.

First Move / Release

Quick catch-to-rip and straight-line go. Jumper starts from a low dip, then rises cleanly without a long pause at the forehead.

Weakness

Feels more like a finisher than a long-possession creator. When you run him off the right side and make him take extra left-hand dribbles into traffic, his shot quality drops.

How To Guard

Top-lock his easy catch, run him off the right side, and close under control so he sees bodies instead of an open launch window.

Confirmed Core

Elijah

Interior finisher / glass pressure / #13

Confirmed jersey: #13. Black jersey big and paint/glass pressure piece.

Tendencies

Looks like the player most likely to turn effort into points: rim runs, second jumps, putbacks, and short catches around traffic.

Hot Zones

Paint, short corner, and both blocks. He becomes dangerous whenever the ball touches the lane twice in the same possession.

First Move / Release

Two-foot gathers, quick inside-hand flips, and power finishes. Not a long wind-up scorer; more of a "catch, seal, finish" big.

Weakness

Most of his value depends on deep catches, second jumps, and broken-floor finishes. Force him to catch a step farther out and make him score through a loaded chest instead of on the move.

How To Guard

Hit him early, keep a body between him and the rim, and finish possessions with a real gang rebound. He feeds on broken structure.

Confirmed Core

Yeyo

Screen-and-rebound big / #77

Confirmed jersey: #77. Big body screener and rebound helper sample.

Tendencies

Big body screener with useful seal value. Helps the group by creating contact, carving space, and collecting second-chance touches.

Hot Zones

Dunker spot, restricted circle, and short-roll catches. Trail threes are possible but feel like a low-volume surprise, not the base diet.

First Move / Release

Wins with position first. Catch, pivot, shoulder into space, then short push finish rather than a high-elevation shot.

Weakness

He is much less dangerous when the touch starts higher or farther from the rim. If his screen turns into a short-roll decision instead of a deep seal, you can live with that more comfortably.

How To Guard

Make him finish over length after the catch and do not let his seal turn into deep paint catches. Weak-side tag must arrive on time.

Confirmed Core

Vince

Veteran connector / calming organizer / #14

Confirmed jersey: #14. Veteran organizer and pace-setter sample.

Tendencies

Looks like a stabilizer who can still score. More patient than frantic, willing to touch the elbow, reset the action, and attack once the floor is balanced.

Hot Zones

Top of key, right slot, and right elbow. The scoring profile looks comfortable when he can see the whole floor before the move starts.

First Move / Release

Eyes stay up. Likes an inside-out or pace dribble into a short rise-up jumper or short decision pass. Shot prep is neat and efficient.

Weakness

He looks best when he can slow the possession down and see the whole floor. If you crowd the handle early and make him take late-clock, deeper, more rushed attempts, the efficiency should come down.

How To Guard

Disrupt the rhythm, not just the finish. Show a body early and crowd the decision window so he cannot dictate pace.

Confirmed Core

Ryan

Forward / pressure piece / #40

Confirmed jersey: #40. old-Ryan is #7 and not charted separately yet.

Tendencies

Looks more useful when he can pressure the lane or arrive on the glass than when he has to create a whole possession from scratch.

Hot Zones

Best threats project around the lane lines, short paint catches, and cleanup range rather than pure standstill perimeter volume.

Weakness

Safer to help off him outside and make him prove clean jumpers or second-side pull-ups before overreacting to the first fake.

How To Guard

Show chest early, tag his rebounding path, and live with contested perimeter attempts if that keeps him out of direct rim pressure.
